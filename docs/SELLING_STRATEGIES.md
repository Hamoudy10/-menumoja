# Selling MenuMoja — Strategies & Profit Math (in Simple Words)

This document explains:
1. What to charge clients and why.
2. Different ways to sell (strategies) with real money examples.
3. How much profit YOU make in each scenario.

---

## Step 1: Understand the numbers that matter

Three numbers control everything:

| Number | What it means | Who decides it |
|---|---|---|
| **Setup fee** | One-time payment when the client starts | You (suggest KES 5,000) |
| **Commission** | % of each sale the system processes | You (suggest 5%) |
| **Monthly cap** | The most the client pays in one month | You (suggest KES 10,000) |

**Why a cap is brilliant:** it makes the deal feel safe ("you'll never pay more than KES 10,000") while protecting you on busy months. And most small restaurants do less than KES 200,000/month in QR orders anyway, so the cap rarely binds — it's mostly a marketing tool.

---

## Step 2: Your cost of running the system (so you never lose money)

Before selling, know what it costs YOU:

| Item | Cost per month (KES) |
|---|---|
| Supabase Pro (database, backups, no pauses) | ~KES 3,500 (~$25) |
| Backend hosting (Render/Railway/VPS) | ~KES 1,000–2,800 |
| Frontend hosting (Vercel free tier) | KES 0 |
| Your time (support) | Your time — set a floor so it's covered |
| **Total** | **~KES 4,500–6,500/month for ALL clients** |

So if you have even **1 active client paying KES 5,000+ commission**, you roughly cover the platform. Everything after that is profit. Keep this in your head when negotiating — a KES 3,000 offer from a client is still fine if it's one of several.

## Step 3: The four ways to sell

### Strategy A — Straight commission (recommended for the first clients)
- KES 5,000 setup, then **5% of sales, capped at KES 10,000/month**.
- Best for: your first 2–5 clients, to build proof and testimonials.

**Example — a small restaurant doing KES 100,000/month in QR orders:**

| | KES |
|---|---|
| Setup fee (once) | 5,000 |
| Commission (5% × 100,000) | 5,000 |
| **Month 1 total** | **10,000** |
| **Months 2+** | **5,000/month** |

Your profit: 5,000 − (share of platform ~1,000) ≈ **KES 4,000/month per client**. With 5 clients ≈ KES 20,000/month profit.

### Strategy B — Flat package (for clients who hate percentages)
- Some owners prefer a fixed price: e.g., **KES 5,000/month, all-inclusive** (setup waived or KES 3,000).
- Best for: busy restaurants who know they'll exceed KES 100,000/month in orders — the cap math makes 5% ≈ the same or more, so the flat fee feels cheaper and is easier for them to budget.

**Compare the two for a busy restaurant (KES 250,000/month in orders):**

| Model | They pay | You earn |
|---|---|---|
| Commission 5% capped | KES 10,000 (capped) | KES 10,000 |
| Flat package | KES 5,000 | KES 5,000 |
| **Client choice:** flat is cheaper for THEM; commission is better for YOU. So sell commission to busy clients, flat to small ones.

### Strategy C — Setup fee + lower commission for pilots
- First 3 months: KES 5,000 setup + **3%** (no cap). Then they convert to the standard 5% capped deal.
- Best for: skeptical clients. The low rate removes the last excuse to say no.
- Example: pilot restaurant doing KES 80,000/month → 3% = KES 2,400/month for 3 months, then 5% (KES 4,000, under cap).

### Strategy D — "Dedicated server" premium (for clients who want their own system)
- KES 5,000–10,000/month + KES 15,000–25,000 setup, running on their own server.
- Best for: hotels, groups, or businesses that want their data on their own machine.
- Your cost: a small VPS ~KES 700–1,000/month + your maintenance time. Big margin.

## Step 4: The honest profit comparison table

Assumptions: platform cost KES 5,500/month (shared), each client's marginal cost to you ≈ KES 500/month (support).

| Clients | Model | Their total cost/month | Your cost/month | **Your profit/month** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 small restaurant (KES 60K sales) | 5% capped | 3,000 | ~500 + 5,500 platform | **−3,000 (loss month 1, then −500)** |
| 3 small restaurants | 5% capped | ~12,000 | ~9,000 | **~3,000** |
| 5 restaurants (mix) | 5% capped | ~25,000 | ~12,000 | **~13,000** |
| 10 restaurants | 5% capped | ~50,000 | ~25,500 | **~24,500** |
| 10 restaurants | Flat KES 5,000 | 50,000 | ~25,500 | **~24,500** (similar) |
| 5 restaurants + 1 hotel (dedicated server) | Premium | ~55,000 | ~27,000 | **~28,000** |

**Key lessons:**
1. You need roughly **2–3 paying clients** before the platform pays for itself.
2. Commission scales beautifully — more sales = more income with zero extra cost to you.
3. The setup fee (KES 5,000 × clients) covers your onboarding time.

## Step 5: Closing scripts (what to say)

**To a skeptical owner:**
> "You don't pay me until it works. We set you up for KES 5,000, and then I only earn 5% of orders your customers place through the QR menu — capped at KES 10,000 a month. If you make KES 100,000 in a month, that's KES 5,000. If you make nothing, you pay nothing. And your M-Pesa money goes straight to YOUR till — I never see your money."

**To a busy owner (who fears percentages):**
> "At your volume the percentage would hit the KES 10,000 cap anyway — so you're effectively paying a fixed KES 10,000 on a great month and less on a slow month. No monthly subscription to worry about when times are quiet."

**To close:**
> "Let's do a 30-day pilot. I'll set everything up this week — QR codes, menu, training. If you don't like it, you keep the menu data and we part ways. You have nothing to lose."

## Step 6: Rules of thumb for the meeting

1. **Never sell on price first** — sell on the problem: "How many menus do you print a year? How long do customers wait to pay? How do you know which dishes make money?"
2. **Demo with their business** — put their actual dishes in the demo menu during the pitch.
3. **Show the money flow** — explain money goes to their till. This builds more trust than anything else.
4. **Always offer a cap** — it sounds generous and protects you too.
5. **Get a testimonial after 30 days** — that testimonial is worth more than a month's commission. Ask for a written one-liner + permission to use their logo.
6. **Never drop below KES 3,000/month equivalent** per client for long — that's around your platform cost divided across few clients.
